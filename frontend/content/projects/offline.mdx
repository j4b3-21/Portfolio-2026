## Problem

Most "wellness" apps are engagement machines: notifications, streaks, scores — attention-economy products wearing a health mask. A walk is the one activity they refuse to leave alone.

## Motivation

The design thesis, stated in the repository: **an AI whose success is measured by how little you need it.** OFFLINE prepares a short route locally, gives occasional spoken cues, and then gets out of the way.

## Technical decisions

- **Everything on-device.** SQLite for history, GPS for the loop, device text-to-speech for cues. No account, no cloud, no signal required to start a prepared experience — hence the name.
- **Deterministic by default.** The Mock planner is a fully offline, deterministic planner built on real inputs (duration, seed, mode, start location). The on-device TinyLlama path exists but stays optional behind the LocalModel boundary.
- **Three modes, not settings pages.** *Quiet*, *Curious*, or *Explore new area* — the interface is a decision, not a configuration panel.
- **Restraint as a feature.** Cues and haptics fire only at meaningful moments; planned landmarks replace ambient cues at most once each.
- **Story reports, not scores.** The report measures things like uninterrupted time and digital intrusion — explicitly OFFLINE-defined experimental metrics, not clinical claims.

## Engineering challenges

Designing for *absence* is harder than designing for presence: throttling cues so they help rather than nag, and route planning that produces a good loop from a single time budget.

## Outcome

**In development** — the full pre-walk → walk → report loop runs on a device; the iOS simulator build is verified; physical-device GPS/TTS/haptics checks are still human-in-the-loop. A behavioral research prototype, not a medical product.

## Lessons learned

Constraints are the design: "offline" and "minimal" weren't limitations imposed on this project — they were the project.
